The Greatest Myth in Learning

It seems totally unfair.

Student A studies for weeks, even hires an expensive tutor, yet gets outperformed by Student B, who only studied for three days.

How is that possible?

One might say, “Well, maybe Student B is just smarter.”

That could be true, but intelligence isn’t the full story. The real difference lies in the greatest myth of learning: The myth that time and effort equal results.

What Separates Good from Bad Students

At school, I saw it all the time.

The top students weren’t the ones constantly stressed, preparing for weeks, hiring tutors, or writing beautiful notes.

It was often the opposite: they were fairly relaxed, had messy notes, and didn’t give up their hobbies even during exam weeks.

So if more time and effort don't guarantee success, what makes the difference?

After talking to many successful students, I noticed a clear pattern from their answers:

Success comes from intention, not just time or effort.

These students all reject the myth of time = results. Instead, they rely on intentional learning, a smarter, more structured approach to studying. I will tell you more on that in just a minute.

The Myth Explained

First, why does this myth persist? Why do so many students still fall for it?

Because we’re not as rational as we think.

Spending hours studying feels productive. It gives a sense of progress and control. It makes us feel like we’ve earned success.

But in reality, without the right methods, that feeling is often an illusion.

It’s like filling a gas tank with olive oil. The tank is full, but the car won’t run. It might even get damaged.

The same happens with students. They study way too hard and get exhausted and stressed - terrible conditions for writing exams.

More time and effort don’t lead to better results. Better methods do.

Intentional Learning

Let’s go back to the gas tank example.

To make a car run, we don’t just fill the tank, we fill it with the right fuel.

Learning works the same way.

When you study, you're putting things into your brain. So before you start, ask:

What exactly do I need to learn?

How can I learn it effectively and efficiently?

With this clarity, you reap great benefits:

You avoid wasting time on things you don’t need.

You use study methods that actually work.

You know you’ve covered everything important.

You stay calm. No need to sacrifice sleep, hobbies, or mental health.

That’s intentional learning. That’s how great students succeed. Not through raw discipline, but through strategy.

An Mindful Way of Studying

You can do this too. Here’s an example:

Imagine you have a math exam next week. This is how you make an intentional study plan.

Clarify what you need to learn:

The exam includes only problems, no theory questions.

The topic is integrals.

Set objectives:

Fully understand integrals.

Practice solving different types of integral problems.

Memorize theory that helps you solve those problems.

Choose methods to fulfil the objectives:

Watch short videos or read articles to fully understand integrals.

Practice all possible types of problems from class and online sources.

If a problem is hard, review the theory until you understand it, then memorize it and practice that type of problem again.

Now you have a focused, efficient study plan.

And this isn’t just hypothetical, this is pretty much how I studied for math exams in my final year. The result: an average of 98% (which got rounded up to 100%).
